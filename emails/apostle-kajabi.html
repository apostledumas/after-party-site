Here are a couple of the questions that came in:

  • "Who has the authority to anoint and elevate people to the office of apostle?"
  • "Isn't an apostle someone who walked with Jesus? If so, why are we calling leaders apostles today?"

Tonight on The After Party, I'm answering all of it.

1. Apostles

Are they still for today? Jesus gave "some, apostles; and some, prophets" to the church "till we all come in the unity of the faith" (Ephesians 4:11-13). We're not there yet. And the Bible names apostles beyond the Twelve, like Barnabas (Acts 14:14).

2. Female apostles

Paul greets Andronicus and Junia, "who are of note among the apostles" (Romans 16:7). We'll talk about what that means for women in ministry today.

3. Teams of apostles

The Holy Ghost said, "Separate me Barnabas and Saul" (Acts 13:2). Apostles were sent together, not alone. That still matters.

4. Apostolic authority

Who recognizes it, who affirms it, and what it's for. Paul said the Lord gave it "for edification, and not for your destruction" (2 Corinthians 10:8).

5. The apostolic inheritance of every believer

Apostle means "sent one." Jesus said, "As my Father hath sent me, even so send I you" (John 20:21). You don't have to become apostolic. It's already yours. It's your inheritance. You just have to tap into it.
