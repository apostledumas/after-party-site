Prophecy, plainly.

God still speaks. Here's what that means, what it doesn't, and why we do it live on YouTube every Thursday.

Maybe you grew up in church and heard the word "prophecy" a hundred times. Maybe you've only heard it in a movie trailer. Either way, you're in the right place. No secret handshake required.

01

What prophecy actually is

Prophecy is God speaking through a person. That's it. It isn't mainly about predicting the future. It's about sharing God's heart with someone, so they know He sees them.

The Bible gives prophecy a simple job description: build people up, cheer them on, bring comfort. If a "word" leaves you crushed, scared or confused, something went wrong.

Scripture 1 Corinthians 14:3

02

Prophecy has some cousins

Prophecy is one of several gifts the Holy Spirit gives. A few others show up a lot on the show:

  • Word of knowledge. God shows you a fact you had no natural way of knowing. A name, a place, a situation.
  • Word of wisdom. God shows you what to do about it. The next step.
  • Discerning of spirits. Sensing what's really going on spiritually, whether something is from God or not.

They're handed out by the same Spirit, to different people, for the good of everyone. Not trophies. Tools for helping people.

Scripture 1 Corinthians 12:7-11

03

Does God still talk today?

Yes. Some people believe these gifts ended when the Bible was finished. We get it, but the Bible itself doesn't say that.

On the very first day of the church, Peter quoted the prophet Joel: in the last days God would pour out His Spirit and ordinary people would prophesy. We're still in those last days. Jesus said His sheep hear His voice, present tense. Paul told the church to eagerly desire prophecy, said "you can all prophesy," and warned them not to treat it with contempt.

Paul does say prophecy will stop one day: when we see Jesus face to face. That hasn't happened yet. And the gifts were given "until we all reach unity in the faith." Look around. We're not there yet either.

Scripture Joel 2:28-29; Acts 2:17-18; John 10:27; 1 Corinthians 14:1, 14:31; 1 Corinthians 13:8-12; Ephesians 4:11-13; 1 Thessalonians 5:19-21

04

Yes, prophets still exist

Prophets didn't vanish with the Old Testament. The New Testament church had them too.

A prophet named Agabus predicted a famine, and it happened. Later he warned Paul about what was waiting for him in Jerusalem. The church in Antioch had prophets and teachers leading together. Judas and Silas, both prophets, spent their time encouraging and strengthening believers. And Jesus Himself gave prophets to the church, right alongside pastors and teachers, to equip people and build them up.

Scripture Ephesians 4:11-12; Acts 11:27-28; Acts 21:10-11; Acts 13:1; Acts 15:32

05

Prophesying, prophetic, prophet

These three get mixed up a lot. Here's a simple way to think about it.

Prophesying

Something any believer can do. Paul said "you can all prophesy." Philip had four daughters who prophesied. You don't need a title to hear God for someone.

Prophetic

Hearing God has become a regular part of your life. You notice His nudges, and you share them often.

Prophet

A calling. A role God gives some people to serve and train the wider church. "Are all prophets?" No. Everyone can prophesy. Not everyone is a prophet.

Scripture 1 Corinthians 14:31; Acts 21:9; 1 Corinthians 12:29; Ephesians 4:11

06

Prophets are not psychics

From the outside it can look similar. Someone tells you something true about your life that they had no way of knowing. But the source and the purpose are completely different.

Different source. The Bible clearly warns against fortune telling, mediums and contacting the dead. Not because they're always wrong, but because they tap the wrong source. In Acts 16 a fortune-telling slave girl followed Paul around saying true things about him. Paul didn't hire her. He cast the spirit out in Jesus' name. Accurate isn't the same as from God.

Different purpose. Prophecy points to Jesus. It's never for sale. When a man named Simon tried to buy the Spirit's power, Peter shut it down hard. Prophecy is never a tool for control, fear or keeping you coming back. It's meant to connect you to God yourself.

Scripture Deuteronomy 18:10-12; Leviticus 19:31; Acts 16:16-18; Acts 8:18-20; Revelation 19:10

07

How to test a word

You don't have to swallow everything someone says just because they said "God told me." The Bible actually tells you to test it. Every time.

  1. Does it line up with the Bible? God won't contradict Himself.
  2. Does it point to Jesus? Or to the person giving it?
  3. Does it build you up? Strengthen, encourage, comfort. That's the target.
  4. What's the fruit? Look at the character of the person and what the word produces over time.

Prophecy comes through humans, and humans miss it sometimes. Some words make sense right away. Some click later. Pray about it, hold on to what's good, and let the rest go.

Scripture 1 John 4:1; 1 Thessalonians 5:20-21; Matthew 7:15-20

08

Made for outsiders

Jesus used this with people who weren't religious at all.

He met a woman at a well and told her about her five husbands. She'd never met Him. She ran back to town saying, "Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did," and many people believed because of what she said. Jesus told a skeptic named Nathanael where he'd been sitting before they ever met. Nathanael went from doubter to "you are the Son of God" in about two sentences.

Paul said the same thing can happen in a room today. An outsider walks in, hears prophecy, the secrets of their heart are laid bare, and they say, "God is really among you!"

Scripture John 4:16-19, 29, 39; John 1:47-49; 1 Corinthians 14:24-25

Quick answers.

Is prophecy just predicting the future?

No. Mostly it's God showing someone they're seen and loved.

Do I have to be a Christian to come on?

No. Come curious. God speaks to people who don't know Him yet.

Does it cost anything?

Never. Prophecy is a gift, not a product.

What if a word doesn't fit?

Pray about it, test it, hold on to what's good. Some words make sense later.

Can I learn to prophesy?

If you follow Jesus, yes. Paul said "you can all prophesy."

Is this a psychic reading?

No. Different source, different purpose. Prophecy always points to Jesus.
